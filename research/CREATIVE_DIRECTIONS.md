# Three experience architectures — before implementation

## 1. The Whole Picture — selected
A photographic exhibition, not a scrolling campaign. Six photographic planes form one sculptural assembly. A visitor selects a program to draw its plane into focus; the overview communicates the breadth of support. Stable navigation and direct detail links keep exploration optional. Internal pages pair a visual exhibit with a readable document, preserving the same frame and scale relationships. Wide geometric typography, cool silver/ultramarine/signal red, authentic image color. Motion assembles and refocuses rather than revealing stacked sections. Mobile reshapes the exhibit into a compact stage above an explicit index.

**Why selected:** directly expresses AOT's whole-person and environmental approach; uses existing varied image sizes honestly; compresses program discovery into one place; radically changes the previous stacked architecture. CSS transforms can make the assembly dimensional without a WebGL download or inaccessible text. Risks: photographic clutter and small text; address with restrained overview geometry, opaque labels and dedicated focus view.

## 2. The Community Edition — rejected
A living civic newspaper with a broad typographic front page, variable-sized stories, a persistent contents margin, issue-like history and evidence supplements. Functional and strong for dense content; low technical cost, excellent mobile reading. Rejected because it risks returning to the earlier editorial language and makes six programs feel like competing news items. Less signature interaction and less architectural distance.

## 3. A Day, Held Together — rejected
A cinematic day viewed through a sequence of ordinary settings: classroom, playground, family, neighborhood. Sideways chapters reveal each program in context, with a scene navigator and complete text index. Warm photographic environments and time-based transitions. Rejected because the available assets cannot honestly sustain a continuous documented day; inventing continuity risks factual context. Mobile/video/performance costs exceed the informational benefit.

## Evaluation (qualitative)
The Whole Picture wins on specificity, originality, visual impact, breadth, performance and distance. Community Edition wins pure reading speed but loses differentiation. A Day, Held Together wins cinematic opportunity but loses authentic asset fit, discoverability and feasibility. All require normal navigation, static HTML and reduced-motion completeness.

**Big idea:** People will remember America On Track because they can bring every side of growing up into the same picture—and see the distinct forms of support behind it. This is an editorial metaphor, not a quantitative diagram of outcomes or a representation of a physical AOT facility.
